# @8ksal8's arming set: two ground oscillations on a tuned Air65, and what the set cannot answer

**Reporter:** @8ksal8, PR #15400 comment of 2026-08-10, archive
`b8_b9_Airmode_on_off_switch_ADRC_PID_btfl_001.zip`, posted as "some logs of arming".
**Craft:** `Board information BEFH BETAFPVG473_V2`, `Craft name AIR65 R`, `motor_kv 1960`,
`acc_1G 2048`, `motorOutput 158,2047`, `vbatcellvoltage 330,345,440` with measured `vbat`
3.41–4.31 V across the set — a 1S pack. That it is a 65 mm whoop is from the craft name and the
reporter, not from the log.
**Firmware:** `c40f1e096` (the b8 base) for log 001, `919116fed` (the b9 tag) for the other four.
**Tune:** his own, not the shipped defaults — `adrcWC 80,80,96`, `adrcWO 103,103,125`,
`adrcB0 7007,4312,5848`, `adrc_gyro_lpf_hz 250`, `adrc_b0_law 1`, `adrc_hover_throttle 27`,
`adrc_liftoff_throttle 40`. This is the tune he reported on 2026-08-09 as flying "just as good or
better than BetaFPV's stock tune".
**Loop and logging:** `looptime 312`, `P interval 4`, 785–806 frames/s saved, `debug_mode 102`
(`DEBUG_ADRC`), `pid_at_min_throttle 1`, `airmode_activate_throttle 25`,
`vbat_sag_compensation 0`.

Every empirical number below is printed by one of the six scripts in this directory, at the
precision shown here; the rounded summary phrases used in prose are generated by `summaries.py`
together with the full-precision values and the named rounding rule behind each, and
`summaries.py --check` is a regression guard over both directions — each phrase must appear
verbatim in every artifact that uses it, with no near-variant of it anywhere in that artifact, and
every measured value in those texts must be traceable to some script's output. It is a guard, not
a proof: its blind spots are listed in the script.
The cell counts quoted for either craft use the firmware's `autoDetectCellCount` arithmetic on the
header's `vbatref` snapshot, which is not a replay of the firmware path — the derivation states
where it differs. Where a figure comes from somewhere else — the reporters' own messages, or
the ADRC-028 write-up — it is labelled at the point of use. See [Reproduction](#reproduction).

## 1. The filenames disagree with the logs

The five files are named for a 2×2×2 experiment — b8 vs b9, Airmode on vs on a switch, ADRC vs
PID. One of those three factors is absent from the recorded data and another is not what the name
suggests.

**All five logs ran ADRC.** Every header carries `pid_type:1`, and
`lookupTablePidType[] = {"CLASSIC", "ADRC"}` (`src/main/cli/settings.c:468`), so 1 is ADRC.
The header is written from the live profile (`blackbox.c:1622` prints
`currentPidProfile->pid_type`), the ADRC output is what overwrites `pidData[].P/I/D`
(`pid.c:1518-1530` via `applyAdrcControl()` at `pid.c:1112-1120`), and the `DEBUG_ADRC` channels
are written only on that path (`adrc.c:711-720`). In the two logs named `PID`, those channels keep
changing to the last frame: `debug[1]` takes 259 distinct values in the first fifth of log 004 and
148 in the last fifth; log 005 gives 249 and 228. A frozen channel would mean the code had stopped
running. It never stopped.

Nor could the profile have been swapped mid-log: stick commands are refused while armed
(`fc/rc_controls.c:244`), MSP changes the PID profile only when disarmed (`msp/msp.c:2853`), and
there is no RC adjustment for it. **There is no classic-PID reference in this corpus.**

## 2. Airmode: which of its two states differed

Airmode has two distinct states in Betaflight and they are easy to conflate.

- `isAirmodeActive` in `fc/core.c:834-843` is latched by throttle: `throttleRaised` only becomes
  true once `throttlePercent` reaches `airModeActivateThreshold` (25 % here), and stays latched until
  disarm. It governs iterm policy and PID stabilisation.
- `isAirmodeEnabled()` (`fc/rc_modes.c:75`) is the feature bit or the BOXAIRMODE switch, with no
  throttle condition at all. **This is the one the mixer uses**: `mixTable()` reads
  `isAirmodeEnabled() || launchControlActive` at `mixer.c:707` and passes it to
  `applyMixerAdjustment()`. With it false and throttle below 0.5, the mixer scales axis authority
  down — 0.5 at zero throttle, rising to 1.0 at half throttle (`mixer.c:685-691`,
  `scaleAirmodeTransition` at `mixer.c:576`). With it true, no such reduction is applied.

The latch state is not logged. In the four ground logs commanded collective `setpoint[3]` is 0 in
every saved frame, so nothing recorded could have armed it — but the first 165.3–169.7 ms are not
recorded either, so this bounds the latch rather than proving it stayed off. In the flight log 003
`setpoint[3]` reaches 1000 (100 %), so there the latch certainly did arm.

The mixer flag is checkable: `FEATURE_AIRMODE` (bit 22 of `features`) is set in 001/002/004 and
clear in 003/005. One caveat on the "off" pair: BOXAIRMODE cannot be read back, because the
decoded mode field is `rcModeActivationMask` and the decoder drops bits above 9 (filed as
blackbox-tools #72/#73). So "off" here means the feature bit is clear, not that the switch was
never flipped.

**Both runaways are in the feature-enabled group.** Counting all five logs by their arm, 2 of the
3 feature-enabled logs failed and 0 of the 2 feature-off ones did, where "failed" means a motor
reaches 2047 during a zero-commanded-collective stretch. A complete split, but of five arms, and
one of the two feature-off logs is the flight rather than a bench arm. That is an observed
association worth testing, not a cause and not a rate.

Times are from the first saved frame. `286`/`283` are single frames and are **not** the amplitude
of the oscillation; the developed oscillation sits at a median of 170.5 and 175.5 °/s with
excursions to 267. Note also that the motor rail comes *after* the 100 °/s crossing, by 12.7 and
20.9 ms — those are different events and were conflated in an earlier draft of this file.

The mean of the four motor outputs is deliberately absent: in a saturated differential mix it
rises with the spread alone. Commanded collective is `setpoint[3]`, and it is zero throughout.

**Frequency.** The blackbox timestamps are not uniformly spaced (median interval 1.265 ms, range
0.621–18.340 ms), so `frequency.py` estimates three ways: Welch on an explicitly resampled grid,
Lomb-Scargle on the real timestamps, and the spacing of prominent time-domain peaks (withheld
unless there are at least 8 intervals with an IQR under 40 % of the median).

| | Welch/128 | Welch/256 | Lomb-Scargle | peak spacing |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 001 roll | 18.89 | 22.04 | 20.99 | 21.08 (n=9) |
| 001 pitch | 18.89 | 22.04 | 20.98 | 21.08 (n=12) |

Welch bin width is 3.07–6.30 Hz at these segment lengths, so a bin centre quoted to two decimals
would report the grid rather than the signal. **Roll and pitch oscillate at about 21 Hz** — the
two timestamp-honest estimators agree within 0.46 Hz per row, and all eight of their values span
20.72–21.66 Hz — **and yaw carries a component near 46 Hz**, for which the peak-spacing estimator returns nothing usable and no amplitude comparison
between axes is offered here. Nyquist sits near 400 Hz, so no alias is needed to explain the tone,
but the stream is decimated and folding cannot be excluded from these logs alone.

**Battery.** `vbat` runs 4.19 → 3.51 V in 001 (min 3.41) and 4.20 → 3.59 V in 002 (min 3.47) at
15–16.5 A on a 1S pack; the quiet arms move 4.23 → 4.22 and 4.19 → 4.18 V. That follows the motor
saturation rather than preceding it, and with `vbat_sag_compensation 0` it does not move
`motorOutputRange`, so it does not distort the numbers above. It does mean these arms are hard on
the pack.

## 4. What is *not* a signature

Closed gate, zero logged `z3` and D-term dominance are present in all four ground logs, including
both quiet ones. The gate is open in **0 of 577, 0 of 534, 0 of 4479 and 0 of 5538** frames — that
is, closed in every frame of all four — and the logged `z3` is zero on all three axes throughout.
`ground_events.py` prints RMS(D)/RMS(P) per axis over two windows:

| log | 0–100 ms R/P/Y | whole log R/P/Y | outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| 001 | 2.24 / 2.12 / 2.47 | 2.03 / 1.92 / 2.08 | runaway |
| 002 | 1.21 / 2.35 / 2.37 | 2.00 / 1.92 / 1.92 | runaway |
| 004 | 2.19 / 1.63 / 2.51 | 1.10 / 0.91 / 2.43 | quiet, 5.553 s |
| 005 | 2.47 / 2.18 / 2.57 | 2.15 / 1.98 / 2.50 | quiet, 6.880 s |

Stated precisely: **in the common 0–100 ms window, `RMS(D)/RMS(P) > 1` on every axis of all four
ground logs**, so "D above P" does not separate the two runaways from the two quiet arms. It is a
property of the control law at this tune (`kd = 2·wc` against `kp = wc²`, plus the observer's
phase), not a marker of failure. The whole-log column is not the basis of that claim and does not
support it on its own — log 004 falls to 1.10 / 0.91 / 2.43 there, with pitch not D-dominant at
all, because most of that log is quiescent.

Separately, and *within* the two runaways, the D-equivalent term is the larger one throughout:
whole-log roll `RMS(D)/RMS(P)` is 2.03 in 001 and 2.00 in 002. That is a real property of the
event. It is simply not diagnostic, because the quiet arms show it too in the matched window.

## 5. `z3` is exactly zero on b9, and conditionally so on b8

The logged field is `lrintf(z3/16)` in an int16. `lrintf` follows the current floating-point
rounding mode — round-to-nearest, ties-to-even by default; it is `lroundf` that rounds halves away
from zero — so a logged zero bounds `|z3| <= 8` and no more. The source closes that gap, and it closes it more
firmly on b9 than on b8.

**b9 (002, 004, 005).** The growth inhibit is keyed on `!liftoff` alone (`adrc.c:649`). The gate is
closed in every saved frame. Nothing sets `liftoff` back to false except the reset at
`adrc.c:391`, and within an armed epoch that is only reached through `adrcResetAll()`, which also
zeroes `z1/z2/z3`; the standalone `adrcResetGate()` call in `pid_init.c` is gated on `!ARMED`. So
either the gate never opened since the arm reset — in which case `z3` has been inhibited from an
exact zero — or it opened and was closed again by a reset that zeroed `z3`. Both branches end at
exactly zero, with no assumption about the unlogged interval.

**b8 (001).** The inhibit additionally requires `throttleAtIdle`, with the idle floor at half of
`adrc_liftoff_throttle` (20 % here). Commanded collective is zero in every recorded frame, but the
first saved frame arrives **167.5 ms after the log's own `Sync beep`** and nothing in that window
is recorded. Had commanded throttle briefly crossed 20 % there, the b8 inhibit would have lifted
and a small `z3` could have grown and then decayed back under the logging quantum. So for 001:

> Every recorded `z3` value is zero, and the internal `z3` was exactly zero **provided** commanded
> throttle stayed below the idle floor from the arm reset onward — which the log cannot confirm
> for its first 167.5 ms.

## 6. Relation to ADRC-028

It is tempting to call this ADRC-028 on a second airframe. The corpus does not support that.
`dedlike_compare.py` re-measures his log with the same estimators used here.

| | ADRC-028 (@dedlike, `btfl_003`) | this set (@8ksal8, 001/002) |
|---|---|---|
| board | DIAT MAMBAF722; `vbatref` 15.37 V, `autoDetectCellCount` arithmetic int(15.37 / 4.30) + 1 → 4S | BEFH BETAFPVG473_V2; `vbatref` 4.22 V, int(4.22 / 4.40) + 1 → 1S |
| size and mass | 5-inch, 612 g with battery — *his own description in the PR thread; the log carries only the board name* | 65 mm — *craft name `AIR65 R` and the reporter* |
| tune | shipped defaults 60/60/60 · 100/100/80 · 2000 | 80/80/96 · 103/103/125 · 7007/4312/5848 |
| record length | 205 frames, 0.211045 s | 577 / 534 frames, 0.715 / 0.679 s |
| dominant axis | yaw | roll and pitch |
| yaw frequency | Lomb 34.66, Welch/128 37.76 Hz (bin width 7.55 Hz) | Welch/256 47.23 / 46.02, Lomb 45.68 / 46.25 Hz |
| roll / pitch frequency | Welch/128 30.21 / 22.66, Lomb 21.24 / 22.41 Hz | ~21 Hz, three estimators agreeing |
| first motor at the upper rail | 127.025 ms | 182.802 / 176.480 ms |
| shared | closed gate, zero `z3`, zero commanded collective, props on, D above P | same |

Two caveats on his column, both printed by the script. His record is 0.21 s, so the frequency
estimators run on a handful of cycles and the spread is correspondingly wide — his write-up's own yaw figure comes from its own analysis, and the roll/pitch numbers here should not be
read as a point estimate. And `axisD[2]` is **absent from his log** — blackbox gated it on the
legacy D-gain, which the shipped defaults leave at 0 on yaw — so any yaw command sum computed from
his log is missing its largest term. The 87.017 ms time-to-limit quoted in
[`pr15400-dedlike-mamba/`](../pr15400-dedlike-mamba/) comes from that write-up's reconstruction,
not from `dedlike_compare.py`.

What is shared is the *situation* — a closed-gate ADRC loop driving props against the ground at
zero commanded collective — and, per section 4, the shared observables do not discriminate failure
from non-failure even inside this one corpus. The defensible claim is **a common susceptibility to
a closed-gate ground loop, on two airframes of different class**. Identity of mechanism is not
established, and the differing axis and frequency are evidence against assuming it.

Three narrower conclusions do hold:

1. **The b9 change had no active path in either recorded episode.** The only control-path
   difference between b8 and b9 replaces the z3-growth inhibit condition
   `!liftoff && throttleAtIdle` with `!liftoff`; in 001 the old condition held for the whole
   recorded stretch, so the new one changes nothing there, and 002 is already b9 and behaves the
   same. The other b9 change is D-term logging. This is not a matched A/B — the two arms are not
   controlled against each other — so it says the fix was irrelevant here, not that nothing else
   about b9 matters.
2. **"The default `b0 = 2000` is miscalibrated" does not cover this.** These `b0` values are
   per-axis and tuned, and the same tune completed the 96.506 s flight in log 003. That refutes the
   simple form of the hypothesis. It does not clear `b0` or loop gain generally: a craft in contact
   with the ground is a different plant, and with the gate shut the observer sees `b0·u = 0` while
   the P and D output terms are still divided by `b0`.
3. **No tested pre-onset observable predicts the outcome.** The closest pair is 002 against 004 —
   same firmware, same tune, same mixer Airmode state — and they differ in outcome. First-100 ms
   observables are close but not identical (|acc| 1.000 vs 1.007 g, mean motor 220.6 vs 188.8, mean
   current 0.822 vs 0.798 A, gyro RMS 6.6 vs 4.2 °/s), and contact preload, prop phase and the
   unlogged 165.3–169.7 ms are not measured at all. "Intermittent" is the honest description;
   "identical starting conditions" would not be.

## 7. The 96.506 s flight (log 003)

It is the control that shows the tune flies, and it contains no ground runaway.

- 96.506 s over 77766 frames; the gate first opens at 2.279 s and is open in 75929 frames.
- `gyroUnfilt` RMS 20.11 / 24.60 / 27.15 °/s roll/pitch/yaw, maxima 132 / 172 / 253 °/s.
- A motor is at 2047 in 550 frames (0.707 %). `vbat` 2.99–4.19 V, peak current 20.20 A.
- The `z3` **debug channel** clips in 40 pitch frames (0.0514 %) and 277 yaw frames (0.3562 %).
  That is the telemetry limit tracked as ADRC-029 — the reconstructed value saturates at
  `|z3| = 524272` — and not the controller's internal clamp. Both factors of the internal clamp are
  recorded: `pidsum_limit` 500 / 500 / 400 against `b0` 7007/4312/5848, i.e. 3503500 / 2156000 /
  2339200, far above the telemetry endpoint. The clipping bounds what any `z3` figure from this log
  can say; it is not evidence of instability.
- In the 18–26 Hz band, over 11 continuous gate-open runs of at least 2 s at 20–40 % collective
  (75.8 s total, PSDs averaged across runs and the metrics derived once from that average), the
  content is 22.54 % of the 8–120 Hz energy on roll and 18.73 % on pitch — with an absolute
  in-band amplitude of **1.038 and 0.877 °/s RMS**. A large fraction of a small number is still a
  small number. The script prints a second window length beside the first: at `nperseg` 512 the
  figures are 22.30 % / 1.036 on roll and 17.30 % / 0.845 on pitch, so the pitch fraction in
  particular is method-sensitive and must not be quoted alone.

With no matched baseline in this corpus, log 003 supports "no runaway, and amplitudes consistent
with his report that it flies well". It does not support a stronger claim of signal cleanliness.

## 8. What this set does and does not establish

**Established.** All five logs are ADRC. Two arms of five entered a self-sustaining,
motor-saturating ground oscillation at about 21 Hz at zero commanded collective, with the gate
closed and no disturbance-estimate growth, on a tune that flies. Within those two events the
D-equivalent term is roughly twice P on roll. The b9 z3 change had no active path in either. Both
runaways occurred with `FEATURE_AIRMODE` set, and neither of the two feature-off logs failed.

**Not established.** That there is a classic-PID comparison here. That Airmode is the cause. That
this is the same mechanism as ADRC-028. That the quiet arms were identical to the loud ones. That
`b0` calibration is exonerated. That anything can be said about the BOXAIRMODE switch state.

**Worth asking for next**, in rough order of value: a genuine `pid_type = CLASSIC` arm on the same
craft and surface; several arms at each Airmode setting rather than one or two, to turn a
complete-split-of-five into a rate; and a props-off arm, which separates the aerodynamic and
ground-contact feedback path from sensor-and-frame coupling at much lower risk than a props-on
repeat.

## Reproduction

```bash
# blackbox_decode from betaflight/blackbox-tools
export BLACKBOX_DECODE=/path/to/blackbox_decode
python3 provenance.py       # firmware, tune, controller, Airmode bit, unlogged head interval
python3 ground_events.py    # amplitudes, saturation, battery, gate/z3, D-vs-P, start conditions
python3 frequency.py        # three independent frequency estimates
python3 flight003.py        # the 96.506 s flight; WELCH_NPERSEG=512 for sensitivity
python3 dedlike_compare.py  # ADRC-028 re-measured with the same estimators
python3 summaries.py        # every rounded phrase used in prose, with its derivation
ARMING_REPLY=<reply.md> python3 summaries.py --check   # enforces both directions of the above
```

Scripts decode the `.bbl.gz` into `_decoded/` on first run; `dedlike_compare.py` also reads
`../pr15400-dedlike-mamba/btfl_003.bbl.gz`. SHA-256 of the decompressed logs is printed by
`provenance.py`; the originals are exactly as posted.
